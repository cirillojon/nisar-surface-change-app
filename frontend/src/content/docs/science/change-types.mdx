## Drivers, not verdicts

Rain and river flow are drawn on the same dates as the NISAR series. With under a year of passes, anything seasonal lines up with anything else seasonal, so the app computes no correlations and names no cause.

What the platform does instead is offer **hypotheses**: a physical driver, its mechanism, the radar channel that shows it, what in the record fits it, what else would, and a test that would tell them apart. For example:

- Okavango's open water was highest on 8 July, months after modelled inflow peaked upstream, with almost no local rain. Hypothesis: a flood pulse from Angola. But: the record starts in late June, so it never sees the water arrive.
- Mexico City's fitted pace is 20 mm/month (Nov–Apr) and 14 (May–Oct). Hypothesis: uncorrected water vapour, which differs between dry and rainy months. Published work found steady rates there and no link to pumping. But: one year cannot rule out a real seasonal change.
- Very dark pixels in the Columbia Basin mirror HV all season: 6.5% in June, 0.6% just after the August HV peak, 11.6% in late October. Hypothesis: bare, smooth fields. But: a flooded field reads the same.

All of them, with their tests: [Physical drivers & hypotheses](/docs/science/hypotheses), and each story in the workspace.
