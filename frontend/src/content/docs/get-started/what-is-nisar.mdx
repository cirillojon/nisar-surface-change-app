**NISAR** (NASA–ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar) launched from Sriharikota, India, on 30 July 2025. It orbits pole to pole, 747 km up. NASA built its L-band radar and 12-metre antenna; ISRO built the spacecraft, an S-band radar and the rocket.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Revisit** | Twice every 12 days (ascending and descending passes) |
| **Coverage** | Nearly all land and ice |
| **L-band** | ~24 cm wavelength; reaches through vegetation |
| **Swath** | ~240 km |
| **Data** | Free, from NASA's Alaska Satellite Facility |

## How radar sees

<Art name="radar" />

A camera needs sunlight. NISAR's radar beams microwaves at the ground and records the echoes, so it sees day and night, through clouds and smoke. "Synthetic aperture" means it combines the echoes along its orbit, as if its antenna were kilometres long.

Rough or upright surfaces (forest, buildings, lava) look bright; calm water and flat fields look dark. Comparing passes shows change down to centimetres. More in [How radar sees Earth](/docs/science/how-sar-works).
